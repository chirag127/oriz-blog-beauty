Most skincare advice tries to sell you steps. The truth is quieter: skin does well with a small number of things done consistently. A shorter routine you finish every day beats a long one you abandon by Thursday.

Here is the whole plan. Four steps, two of them optional on most days.

## The four steps

Skincare has a natural order, and the order is the routine:

1. **Cleanse** — remove the day (or night) so the next steps can work.
2. **Treat** — one active, if you have a specific goal.
3. **Moisturise** — hold water in the skin barrier.
4. **Protect** — sunscreen, every morning, no exceptions.

That is it. If you only ever did cleanse, moisturise, and sunscreen, your skin would be fine. The "treat" step is where people over-buy.

## Morning

In the morning you are protecting, not repairing. Keep it light.

- **Rinse or gentle cleanser.** If your skin is dry, plain water is enough at dawn. If it is oily, a mild gel cleanser.
- **Moisturiser.** A simple lotion or cream. Look for glycerin or a humectant near the top of the ingredient list.
- **Sunscreen.** Broad-spectrum, SPF 30 or higher, roughly two finger-lengths for the face and neck. This is the single highest-value step in any routine. It prevents most visible ageing and lowers skin-cancer risk.

Three steps, under two minutes.

## Night

At night you clean off the day and let the skin repair while you sleep.

- **Cleanse properly.** If you wore sunscreen or makeup, this matters. A gentle cleanser removes the film so your moisturiser is not sitting on top of grime.
- **Treat (optional).** This is the one active slot. Most people should pick *one* of: a retinoid for texture and fine lines, or a vitamin C in the morning for brightness, or a gentle exfoliating acid two or three nights a week. Do not stack all three at once — that is how you wreck your barrier.
- **Moisturise.** A slightly richer cream than the morning one is fine.

## What "one active" means

The biggest beginner mistake is buying five treatment products and using them all, every night, immediately. Actives are powerful precisely because they change how skin behaves — so you introduce them slowly.

Start one new active at a time. Use it twice a week for two weeks. If your skin is calm, go to every other night. If it stings, flakes, or turns red, you moved too fast — pull back, moisturise, and wait until the barrier settles.

A retinoid is the most evidence-backed single active for most goals. If you only add one thing, add that, at night, starting low.

## Patch test before you commit

Before a new product joins the routine, put a small amount on the inside of your forearm or behind the ear for two or three days. If nothing reacts, move it to the face. This costs you three days and saves you a week of an angry face.

## Signs your routine is too much

Your skin will tell you when you have over-done it:

- Tightness or a stinging feeling after products go on
- New flaking or rough patches
- Redness that was not there before
- Breakouts in places you do not normally break out

The fix is almost always the same: stop the actives, go back to cleanse–moisturise–protect, and let the barrier rebuild for a week or two. Skincare is not a competition. The barrier — the outer layer that holds water in and irritants out — is the thing you are protecting. Everything else serves it.

## The routine, on a card

- **AM:** rinse → moisturise → sunscreen
- **PM:** cleanse → (one active) → moisturise

Stick that somewhere you will see it. Do it for a month before you judge whether it works. Consistency, not the number of bottles, is what moves skin.
